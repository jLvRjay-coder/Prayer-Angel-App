## Research inventory and case count

The original 366-page PDF has twelve source packets including the introductory comparison. Those packet headings do not define the publication count. The general plural marriage packet supports two distinct cases, Kirtland and Nauvoo and early Utah. Cases 009 and 010 expand it with identified primary documents and research. Available branch summaries were consulted; this release does not claim full review of unretrieved branch manuscripts. All twelve numbered case manuscripts are now published; the closing guide remains separate.

## Publication sequence

The LDS cases and separate closing Christian conversation guide are published. Islam research can now begin as its own investigation. A whole-collection editorial review remains before book adaptation. Each subsequent religious collection should also end with a conversation guide. Historical observations, the religion's self-description, and the project's evangelical and Trinitarian Christian assessment remain distinguishable.

## Future book adaptation

A possible first volume is The Belief Files LDS and Biblical Christianity, with the introduction as the opening comparison, Cases 001 through 012 adapted as chapters, and the conversation guide as the closing practical section. This is a proposed structure, not a finished book or a publication commitment.

Retain editable chapter manuscripts, numbered source registers, qualifications, discussion questions, and a record of sources reviewed. Before book publication, reconcile chapter overlap, add transitions and a glossary, review quotations and permissions, and obtain a fuller historical and theological source review. Treat other religions as later volumes or collections once their own research is complete. Page count, publisher, pricing, and release date remain undecided.

Updated November 8, 2026.

Current release: the separate Christian conversation guide, completing the fourteen-resource LDS collection. The native guide follows Case 012 and keeps its own eight-section reader, source register, and PDF. It includes openings for friends, family, and missionaries; shared terminology; the gospel; common questions; careful use of the investigations; illustrative conversations; and follow-up. Its perspective is explicitly evangelical and Trinitarian, while official LDS sources establish LDS self-description. The conversations are examples, not claimed testimony. Its reusable framework is retained for future collections. The source register for Case 005 includes selected 2025 and 2026 research; Case 006 distinguishes early records, retrospective accounts, current authority categories, and formal healing practice.

Case 007 distinguishes 1845 publications, later recollections about Smith, institutional affirmation, and the difference between a claim of revelation and a surviving formal revelation document. Its biblical comparison retains maternal imagery, divine offspring language, and the context of adoption and marriage.

Case 008 separates persecution, historical blood-atonement preaching, the Mountain Meadows crime, disputed responsibility and knowledge, Native testimony, and later acknowledgment. It distinguishes a published sermon from a proved execution, local LDS responsibility from an unproved direct order from Young, and historical teaching from the current Church position. The source register identifies the published letter transcription and the specific deposition page checked without claiming a full trial-file audit.

Case 009 distinguishes original documents, contemporary copies, later typescripts, participant recollections, first-wife consent provisions, and the exemption in Doctrine and Covenants 132. Reports of Smith's angel are not independent observations of the angel. Helen Mar Kimball's age, retrospective testimony, and the limits on reconstructing private relationships remain explicit.

Case 010 distinguishes household population from plural husbands and lifetime exposure from residence at a date. Women's public defenses, Wells's private diary, and Stenhouse's criticism retain their different purposes and limits. Demographic models are not censuses, and larger male family totals do not establish a reproductive benefit for each wife.

Case 011 distinguishes new unions, continuing marital relations, support, and sealing claims. It preserves the document/publication/acceptance dates, limits the Senate review to identified passages, identifies Smoot as a monogamist, and distinguishes individual leadership approval from collective action. Original ledger and disciplinary files were not comprehensively audited.

Case 012 includes Black women's temple restrictions as well as men's ordination, early Black priesthood, James's own petition, authoritative explanations, and the 1978 change. June 1, June 8, June 9, and September 30 describe distinct stages. Current disavowal is not silently turned into a formal apology. Transmitted statements are identified, and the Christian standard is also applied to Christian institutions.
